## 1. Research Purpose

Phase 1 establishes the **evidence base** for all subsequent brand, design, and architecture decisions. No assumptions are treated as facts. No user research is invented.

All claims are labeled:
- `[OBSERVED]` — Directly seen in the interface or source
- `[INFERRED]` — Reasonable interpretation of observed evidence
- `[HYPOTHESIS]` — Proposition requiring validation
- `[EVIDENCE]` — Supported by citable external source

## 3. Competitor Selection

Selected platforms cover a deliberate range of tiers and focus areas:

| # | Platform | Tier | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Peloton** | Premium | Connected fitness, content subscription |
| 2 | **Nike Training Club** | Mainstream / Free | Brand-led workout content |
| 3 | **Apple Fitness+** | Premium / Ecosystem | Integrated wearable + video coaching |
| 4 | **Whoop** | Premium / Data-first | Performance tracking, recovery science |
| 5 | **Freeletics** | Mid-tier / AI-led | Bodyweight training, AI coaching |
| 6 | **Strava** | Community / Tracking | Activity tracking, social motivation |

**Rationale:**
- Peloton represents cinematic, high-production fitness content — the closest reference to PREDYX's aspirational aesthetic.
- Nike Training Club shows how a major brand delivers free premium content — relevant to brand authority.
- Apple Fitness+ shows ecosystem-integrated premium fitness at scale.
- Whoop represents performance data and recovery science — relevant to PREDYX's tracking hypothesis.
- Freeletics shows AI coaching and progressive personalization without heavy hardware dependency.
- Strava shows community motivation and social progress tracking.

## 4. Analysis Dimensions

For each competitor, the following dimensions are evaluated where evidence exists:

1. Product proposition
2. Information architecture
3. Homepage structure and first impression
4. Onboarding
5. Workout / program discovery
6. Exercise library and instruction
7. Workout experience
8. Progress tracking
9. Membership / subscription model
10. Mobile vs. desktop behavior
11. Navigation patterns
12. Interaction patterns and micro-interactions
13. Visual language (color, type, imagery, density)
14. Motion and animation approach
15. Trust-building patterns
16. Accessibility signals
17. Performance considerations visible from the experience

---

## 5. User Journey Scenarios

Three primary scenarios will be mapped as hypotheses:

| Scenario | Description |
|---|---|
| **S1: Pre-Workout** | Discovery → motivation → workout/program selection → preparation |
| **S2: During Workout** | Session navigation → exercise instruction → progression → tracking |
| **S3: Post-Workout** | Completion → summary → progress update → recovery → re-engagement |

---

## 6. Evidence Standards

| Standard | Rule |
|---|---|
| No fabrication | Never invent interviews, surveys, statistics, or user behavior |
| Source citation | Record the source for every important external claim |
| Primary preferred | Use official product websites and documentation first |
| Label uncertainty | Label anything not directly observed |
| Marketing ≠ evidence | Marketing claims require corroboration before being treated as evidence |
